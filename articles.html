The basic idea

What is arbitrage?

Arbitrage attempts to benefit when the same or a related asset trades at different prices in different places. A trader buys where it is cheaper and sells where it is more expensive. The displayed difference is a gross spread—not guaranteed profit.

Simple illustration

If an asset costs $100.00 on one market and can be sold for $100.80 on another, the visible spread is $0.80. Fees, slippage, transfer time, changing prices, and failed orders can reduce it or turn it into a loss.

Common types

  • Cross-exchange arbitrage compares two venues.
  • Triangular arbitrage compares exchange rates among three assets.
  • DEX arbitrage compares liquidity pools or a pool and a centralized venue.
  • Statistical arbitrage uses models and carries model and directional risk.
Context

A short history

Merchants have used regional price differences for centuries. Telegraphs, electronic markets, and algorithms made differences smaller and shorter-lived. Crypto markets are fragmented across around-the-clock venues, networks, and liquidity pools. This can create spreads, but competition, costs, and execution risk remain significant.

The workflow

How an arbitrage bot works

  1. Observe: collect current quotes and available liquidity.
  2. Compare: estimate an executable spread rather than relying on a displayed last price.
  3. Filter: subtract expected fees, slippage, network cost, and a safety margin.
  4. Control: enforce approved assets, allocations, limits, and strategy rules.
  5. Execute: submit orders only when the opportunity passes the checks.
  6. Reconcile: verify fills and fees and record results in the portfolio ledger.

Realistic expectations

What profit can you make?

No fixed profit can be promised. Results depend on capital, opportunity frequency, liquidity, speed, fees, slippage, failed trades, limits, and losses. A strategy target is a configuration goal—not a forecast or guarantee.

Before funding

Important risks

  • Execution: price or liquidity may change before every order fills.
  • Counterparty: an exchange, custodian, token, bridge, or pool may fail.
  • Technology: software, APIs, smart contracts, networks, and data can malfunction.
  • Market: hedges can be incomplete and volatile assets can fall quickly.
  • Operational: wrong assets, networks, addresses, permissions, or decisions can cause loss.
  • Legal and tax: eligibility and obligations differ by location and can change.
You can lose some or all of the capital used.

Only use funds you can afford to lose. Read the risk disclosure and obtain independent advice where appropriate.
